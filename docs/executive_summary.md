# Executive Summary

## Decision

A DRG-based funding structure is materially more sustainable than a flat
per-diem structure for a hospital portfolio with complex casemix. Indexation
alone does not compensate for the loss exposure created when long, high-cost
episodes are reimbursed at a fixed daily rate.

## Evidence

The reproducible demo models 50,000 privacy-safe synthetic episodes calibrated
to national Australian hospital distributions.

| Scenario | Three-year revenue | Three-year margin |
|---|---:|---:|
| DRG-based, 5% indexation | A$1.542B | **A$91.3M** |
| DRG-based, 3% indexation | A$1.483B | A$32.3M |
| Per-diem, 5% indexation | A$637.1M | -A$772.3M |
| Per-diem, 3% indexation | A$612.7M | **-A$796.7M** |

The year-on-year revenue bridge also shows that headline growth should not be
interpreted as pricing success:

- Total revenue change: **+A$155.8M**
- Volume effect: **+A$159.2M**
- Casemix effect: **-A$3.6M**
- Rate effect: **+A$0.2M**
- Residual: **A$0**

Most reported growth is therefore activity-led rather than rate-led.

## Recommended Actions

1. Use DRG-based reimbursement as the negotiation anchor for complex acute
   activity.
2. Define explicit stop-loss and outlier provisions rather than relying on
   indexation to absorb high-cost episodes.
3. Prioritise DRGs by absolute margin exposure and margin per separation before
   each fund negotiation.
4. Track price, volume and casemix separately in monthly commercial reporting.
5. Use the scenario modeller live with Health Funds stakeholders to document
   assumptions and compare counteroffers consistently.

## Governance

IHACPA, AIHW and APRA provide the public national calibration inputs.
Patient-level records and contract terms are synthetic. The model is a
decision-support demonstration and does not represent actual hospital-group
financial performance.
